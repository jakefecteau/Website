About Me

How I got interested in investing, and what keeps me interested.

My Story

I grew up in Holbrook, on Long Island. I attended St. Anthony’s High School in Melville, where I was part of the Leadership Team and rowed varsity crew.

I’m now studying Business Management with a Finance specialization at Stony Brook University, and I expect to graduate in January 2027.

Why Investing

I started getting interested in investing when I was 16.

The idea of compounded growth really excited me.

My parents first introduced me to compounding. I also went on golfing trips every year with about 50 men in their 50s and 60s. I often asked them for one thing they wished they had done when they were younger. Almost every time, they said they wished they had invested earlier.

On my 18th birthday, I opened my own brokerage account.

What I’ve Learned

Investing has taught me that time in the market is much more important than trying to time the market.

I’ve also learned that a diversified portfolio is important for getting through tough times in the market, and that it’s important to stay consistent with investing.

What I Follow

Capital markets interest me, and I follow the tech industry closely.

I’ve been following Nvidia very closely for several years. I first invested in 2022, while looking for new companies to invest in. AI was starting to take off, and I realized the data centers behind it ran on Nvidia’s GPUs, so I figured the company could have a lot of growth potential.

In summer 2026, I interned in wealth management at Main Street Financial Group, where I supported portfolio analysis and investment research across stocks, ETFs, and mutual funds.

Projects

School · BUS 356 · Excel

Gaming Company Acquisition Analysis

The question: Should a software firm acquire one of four gaming companies or start its own?

What I did: Built out projected cash flows for all five options and compared them using discounted cash flow, net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), and payback period.

Outcome: I recommended acquiring Action Games, which had the highest NPV (about $201 million) and IRR (about 44.6%). It also required the largest initial investment and had the longest payback period, about 5.7 years.

School · BUS 340 · Excel, SQL, Tableau

College Earnings Analysis

The question: How do bachelor’s graduates’ earnings differ by school, field of study, and years after graduation?

What I did: Wrote Excel formulas to restructure earnings data, loaded it into a SQL data warehouse, and built Tableau dashboards comparing Stony Brook University, Georgia Tech, and Salem State University.

Outcome: In communication, computer science, mathematics, social sciences, and visual arts, Georgia Tech graduates had higher median earnings than graduates of the other schools with data. Based on earnings, I recommended it as the strongest of the three in these fields.

School · BUS 356 · Excel

Energy Stock Screening Model

The question: Which of seven energy stocks was the strongest buy under a rules-based model?

What I did: Compared XOM, OXY, ECA, CNQ, APA, APC, and DVN on liquidity, efficiency, leverage, profitability, and valuation ratios, using the assignment’s scoring rules to turn each result into points.

Outcome: I recommended ECA, which scored highest with 8 points, just ahead of XOM and OXY with 7 each. This was a class exercise, not current investment advice.
