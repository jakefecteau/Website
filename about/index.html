About Me

Where I come from, how I got interested in investing, and where I’m headed.

My Story

I grew up in Holbrook on Long Island and attended St. Anthony’s High School in Melville, where I was part of the Leadership Team and spent four years as a coxswain on the varsity crew team. My job was to steer the boat and keep the rowers in sync. I learned how to give a clear call under pressure, especially late in a race when everyone was tired.

I’ve also worked in retail and event operations, including as an operations lead. Tracking inventory, coordinating projects, and finding better ways to handle recurring tasks gave me a firsthand look at how a business runs day to day.

I’m now studying Business Management with a specialization in Finance at Stony Brook University and expect to graduate in January 2027. Courses like Investment Analysis, Financial Management, and Mergers and Acquisitions have given me the tools to explore questions I first had as an investor. How do you decide what a company is worth? When does it make sense for one business to acquire another?

Why Investing

I first became interested in investing and the broader financial markets when I was 16. I learned about compounded growth, and I was fascinated by how the returns on an investment could generate returns of their own over time.

Each year, I went on a golf trip with a group of about 50 guys, most of them in their 50s and 60s. Out on the course, I’d often ask what they wished they’d done when they were my age. The answer I heard most often was that they wished they’d started investing sooner.

I took that advice to heart. On my 18th birthday, I opened my own brokerage account.

What I’ve Learned

Managing my own portfolio has taught me patience. When a stock I own drops, I go back to the reason I bought it and ask whether anything about the business has changed. That habit helps me keep a long-term perspective when it’s tempting to react to a short-term swing.

I’ve also learned to think about how each holding fits into my portfolio. Even when I feel strongly about a company, I want to understand how much depends on that one investment doing well. That’s why diversification matters to me. I also try to invest consistently, especially when it’s tempting to wait for the perfect time to buy.

What I Follow

I’m interested in how companies raise capital and how investors decide which businesses to back. Technology is the industry I follow most closely. In 2022, I started looking at the computing power needed to run AI models. That led me to Nvidia and the GPUs it supplied to data centers. I thought growing demand for that hardware could drive the company’s growth, and I made my first investment in Nvidia that year.

In the summer of 2026, I worked as a wealth management intern at Main Street Financial Group. I entered and verified client portfolio data in YCharts, supported portfolio analysis and research across stocks, ETFs, and mutual funds, and helped prepare reports for advisors. Working on those reports showed me how much an advisor’s understanding of a portfolio depends on getting the details of each holding right.

Projects

School · BUS 356 · Excel

Gaming Company Acquisition Analysis

The question: Should a software firm acquire one of four gaming companies or start its own?

What I did: Built out projected cash flows for all five options and compared them using discounted cash flow, net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), and payback period.

Outcome: I recommended acquiring Action Games, which had the highest NPV (about $201 million) and IRR (about 44.6%). It also required the largest initial investment and had the longest payback period, about 5.7 years.

School · BUS 340 · Excel, SQL, Tableau

College Earnings Analysis

The question: How do bachelor’s graduates’ earnings differ by school, field of study, and years after graduation?

What I did: Wrote Excel formulas to restructure earnings data, loaded it into a SQL data warehouse, and built Tableau dashboards comparing Stony Brook University, Georgia Tech, and Salem State University.

Outcome: In communication, computer science, mathematics, social sciences, and visual arts, Georgia Tech graduates had higher median earnings than graduates of the other schools with data. Based on earnings, I recommended it as the strongest of the three in these fields.

Energy Stock Screening Model

The question: Which of seven energy stocks was the strongest buy under a rules-based model?

What I did: Compared XOM, OXY, ECA, CNQ, APA, APC, and DVN on liquidity, efficiency, leverage, profitability, and valuation ratios, using the assignment’s scoring rules to turn each result into points.

Outcome: I recommended ECA, which scored highest with 8 points, just ahead of XOM and OXY with 7 each. This was a class exercise, not current investment advice.
